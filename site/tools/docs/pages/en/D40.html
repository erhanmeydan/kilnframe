Kilnframe uses AI in three ways: models that run on your own computer, the AI model you connect for the writing and the judging, and a few services with keys of their own for sound. Nothing goes anywhere you did not choose.

On your computer

These run on this computer. Nothing is sent and nothing is paid:

  • the transcript (unless you choose cloud transcription);
  • cutting pauses and filler words;
  • finding the person in the frame, to follow them or to put them in front of an animation;
  • voice isolation, noise reduction and echo reduction;
  • finding faces to blur, and searching your footage by a description.

Some of them need a model file the first time, such as the 16 MB voice model. It is downloaded only when you start that feature.

With your AI model

The AI model you choose in Settings › AI does the work that needs judgement or writing:

  • finding retakes, asides and similar passages;
  • writing animations;
  • AI colour;
  • captions, B-roll suggestions, short clips, publishing texts and translations;
  • stories from a topic, and the Assistant.

It runs on your own account: your Claude or ChatGPT subscription (how), an API key, or a model on your own computer with LM Studio or Ollama. Kilnframe sells no credits and counts no minutes.

With a key of its own

  • Music and story narration: Google Gemini, or Eleven Music with an ElevenLabs key.
  • Say Again in Voice and sound effects from text: ElevenLabs.
  • Cloud transcription, if you turn it on: ElevenLabs, OpenAI or Google Gemini.

A job that costs money on one of these asks before it starts.

What is sent

For an AI job, only the text and the small frames that job needs (the transcript, your note, stills) go to the model you chose. Your videos and projects stay on your computer. With a model on your own computer, nothing leaves it. Your keys are kept in the system keychain and never sent to us. The privacy policy lists everything that leaves the app.

Every step can be undone

What an AI job changed shows in What the AI just did, a row with an Undo button. Each change is a step in History too, and Undo takes it back like any of your own.
